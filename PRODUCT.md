# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Parichay is for people who want a calm way to explain how they communicate, relate, work, and set boundaries without creating an account or storing data on a server.

The priority wedge is Gen Z and Millennial relationships: talking stages, close friendships, new roommates, early collaborators, situationships, partners, chosen family, and anyone dealing with misread texts or crossed expectations. Work introductions and hard conversations remain supported, but the product should feel necessary first when someone thinks, "I do not want to explain my whole deal again."

## Product Purpose

Parichay is a privacy-first web application for creating a private intro page. It helps users answer guided questions, choose what is included, private, or omitted, and turn the useful parts into a shareable artifact.

The current UI is organized around four starting points:

- Work: for a manager, teammate, client, or collaborator who needs the useful version.
- Someone close: for someone who cares and wants fewer wrong guesses.
- Hard conversation: for a note before something the user does not want to fumble.
- Two of us: for two people trying to stop explaining the same thing twice.

Success means the user can name important context once, control what leaves the page, and send something that feels useful in a real relationship.

## Brand Relationship

Parichay is a sibling brand to Reflections. Both share calm, human, privacy-first principles. Parichay is distinct as a structured intro-page studio, not a journal, diary, sanctuary, mood tracker, insight dashboard, HR assessment, chatbot, AI product, or therapy product.

Primary brand docs live in `docs/brand/`.

## Brand Personality

Warm, calm, human, careful, and practical. The interface should feel like a premium private document studio rather than a clinical form builder, generic AI product, productivity dashboard, or self-optimization tool.

For Gen Z and Millennial relationship use, the product should feel emotionally precise and socially useful, not trend-chasing. It should help users avoid misreads, share boundaries, explain patterns without overexplaining, and make difficult context easier to send.

## Canonical Language

Use these product terms consistently: intro, intro page, manual, answer, visibility, Share, Private, Hide, included, private, omitted, artifact, memory only, save in link, share link, QR code, export, sample intro.

Controls may say `Share`, `Private`, and `Hide`. Explanatory copy should prefer `included`, `private`, and `omitted`.

Prefer "intro page" for the first-time-user explanation and navigation. Use "manual" when referring to the composed artifact or mode names already present in the product, such as Me Manual and Work Manual.

## Anti-references

Avoid clinical healthcare styling, manipulative coaching patterns, generic SaaS dashboards, purple-blue AI gradients, glassy panels, decorative metric cards, gamified progress loops, and interfaces that make privacy feel technical or intimidating.

Avoid copy that says or implies diagnosis, healing, scoring, optimization, magic, unlocking, AI authority over the user's identity, or personality typing.

## Design Principles

- Privacy is visible and understandable at every step.
- The product should lower emotional friction, not add ceremony.
- Writing should feel like composing a useful note, not filling a database.
- Controls should use plain language and familiar affordances.
- Calm visual craft matters because users are writing sensitive context.
- Omitted information is a valid choice, not a failure state.
- The finished intro is the outcome; any generation or formatting help is secondary.
- Gen Z and Millennial relevance should come from usefulness in real relationship moments, not slang, memes, or decorative trend signals.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, semantic structure, and screen reader names. The interface should support touch users with 44px minimum targets, reduced motion preferences, long text, and users who need privacy information stated explicitly rather than implied by color alone.
